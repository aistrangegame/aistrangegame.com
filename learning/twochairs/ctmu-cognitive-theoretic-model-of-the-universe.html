The CTMU

Two chairs

Christopher Langan's Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe claims reality must be a self-describing, self-processing language — and that this explains why anything exists at all.

The question

In 2002, Christopher Langan published the Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe in the journal of an intelligent-design society, arguing reality must be a self-describing, self-processing language in order to exist at all. It has not appeared in a mainstream, peer-reviewed physics or philosophy journal, and most academics have not engaged with it. Is that neglect, or is the theory built so it can't be tested?

Round 1
The chair that takes the CTMU seriouslyLangan's core argument: for reality to be knowable at all, its own structure must include the means of representing and processing itself — otherwise there's no way for a describable universe to exist as one.
The chair that calls it unfalsifiableThe CTMU has not been published in a mainstream, peer-reviewed physics or philosophy journal, and its central claims look true by the definition of its own terms, not by any test anyone could run.
Round 2
The chair that takes the CTMU seriouslyPlenty of real theories started outside the mainstream and stayed there a long time before being tested properly — obscurity by itself isn't evidence a theory is wrong, only that it hasn't been checked.
The chair that calls it unfalsifiableA theory built to prove its own conclusion — that a self-describing universe exists because only a self-describing universe could be described — is hard to distinguish from a closed loop of definitions dressed as physics.
Round 3
The chair that takes the CTMU seriouslyIt asks a question physics mostly declines — why there is a describable, law-governed universe at all — and asking is worth something, even short of an answer.
The chair that calls it unfalsifiableBeing ignored isn't proof of being wrong, but decades without one confirmed, checkable prediction is a long time for a theory of everything to produce nothing anyone outside it can test.

Held open

What's checkable: the CTMU has not appeared in a mainstream peer-reviewed physics or philosophy journal, and academic engagement with it has been minimal. What isn't checkable: whether that reflects a flaw in the theory or a blind spot in the field. No experiment currently distinguishes between the two.

A theory that explains everything is hard to test.
