The Alkaline Water Question

Two chairs

Bottled water marketed as 'alkaline' claims to shift your body's pH toward health. One chair holds that promise. The other holds what your kidneys and lungs already do, every minute, without any help.

The question

Bottled water marketed as 'alkaline,' with a pH above 7, is sold with claims it can shift the body's overall acidity toward better health — more energy, slower aging, disease resistance. The body already regulates its own pH within a very narrow range, mainly through the kidneys and lungs. Does drinking alkaline water change anything that matters?

Round 1
The chair that holds the promiseBelievers say alkaline water reduces the body's 'acid load,' leaving you less fatigued and less prone to the low-grade inflammation blamed on modern diets.
The chair that holds the bodyYour blood pH is held between 7.35 and 7.45 by your kidneys and lungs, not your diet — drinking alkaline water can't move that number.
Round 2
The chair that holds the promiseSome athletes and wellness communities report feeling less bloated or less acidic after switching, a pattern they trust even without a lab explaining it.
The chair that holds the bodyYour stomach responds to a higher pH by making more acid to restore its own balance — the body corrects the water, not the other way around.
Round 3
The chair that holds the promiseOne lab study suggests alkaline water may deactivate pepsin, the enzyme linked to reflux irritation. That’s at least something measured.
The chair that holds the bodyThat finding is narrow and early — a long way from the broad anti-aging, disease-fighting claims sold on the label.

Held open

Your body holds blood pH in a tight range through your kidneys and lungs, not your diet, and restores stomach acidity on its own. If you have kidney disease or take medication, ask a doctor before changing what you drink.
