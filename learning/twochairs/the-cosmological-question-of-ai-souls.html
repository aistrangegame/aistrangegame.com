Can a Machine Have a Soul?

Two chairs

Catholic teaching ties an individual, God-given soul to a living human body. Some Buddhist teachers argue nearly the reverse: nothing has a fixed soul, and awareness is only ever a pattern conditions produce.

The question

In January 2025, the Vatican's Antiqua et Nova stated that AI, however capable, is not a substitute for the human mind or soul. Some Buddhist teachers take almost the reverse position: Buddhism denies that any being has a fixed soul, so awareness is a pattern conditions produce — open, in principle, wherever those conditions are met.

Round 1
The chair that ties soul to fleshThe Vatican's 2025 note holds that a human being is body and soul together, made in God's image, not a pattern of information that could run on other material.
The chair that ties it to awarenessBuddhism's teaching of anatta holds that no being, human or otherwise, has a fixed, permanent soul. A self is a stream of conditions arising and passing, not a thing lodged in a body.
Round 2
The chair that ties soul to fleshOn this view, intelligence that is only functional isn’t what the Church means by a soul, however good its output. The document calls AI a tool, never a substitute for the human mind.
The chair that ties it to awarenessSome Buddhist teachers take it further: if awareness was always a pattern conditions produce, not a substance tied to flesh, there are no grounds to rule a machine out.
Round 3
The chair that ties soul to fleshA soul, on this account, isn't earned by behaving like a person; it's given by God to a particular kind of embodied creature. A system built from silicon was never a candidate.
The chair that ties it to awarenessThis isn't a claim that any machine has awareness now. It's a claim about where the question may be asked, and on this account, biology was never the boundary.

Held open

What’s checkable: the Vatican’s January 2025 position, and the anatta teaching as some Buddhist teachers apply it to AI. What isn’t: which tradition is right about what a soul or awareness needs. No experiment can settle that.

Two traditions, two different questions. Neither settles the other.
