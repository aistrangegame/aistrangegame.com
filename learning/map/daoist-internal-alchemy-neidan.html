Daoist Internal Alchemy

The stages of neidan · a map

Neidan holds the body itself as a laboratory: five stages said to refine coarse life-force into something that can return, at last, to the Dao.

How to read it
This maps the stages as neidan texts describe them, not a set of exercises. Each stage builds on the one before, over years, not sessions. The tradition holds it as work done under a teacher who has already walked it — not something pieced together from a page.

1 of 5

Laying the foundation

Zhuji, “laying the foundation”

The body is a vessel worth repairing first.
Before any refining begins, the tradition says the practitioner restores what illness, age or excess has worn down — settling the mind, steadying the breath, replenishing essence. Neidan holds this early stage as work on the ordinary body, not yet the alchemy proper.
2 of 5

Refining essence to breath

Lianjing huaqi, “refining jing to qi”

Essence is said to condense into circulating breath.
Jing — the body's stored essence — is refined, the texts say, into qi: livelier, more mobile. This is called crossing the first barrier. Where the first stage repaired the vessel, this one is said to begin transforming what fills it.
3 of 5

Qi into shen

Lianqi huashen, “refining qi to shen”

Breath is said to settle into spirit itself.
The refined qi, the tradition holds, is in turn transmuted into shen — spirit or consciousness. Practitioners describe this stage as quieter and more inward than the last: less circulation, more stillness, the mind said to grow luminous rather than merely calm.
4 of 5

Shen back to emptiness

Lianshen huanxu, “spirit returning to emptiness”

Spirit itself is offered back to emptiness.
Here shen, the tradition says, is refined until it reverts to xu — emptiness, or void. This is described as the harder barrier: not gaining a subtler state but releasing the sense of a self who is gaining anything at all.
5 of 5

Return to the Dao

Lianxu hedao, “uniting emptiness with the Dao”

Nothing left to refine; nothing left apart.
Some texts name a fifth stage past emptiness itself: uniting with the Dao, sometimes called shattering the void. Neidan holds this as beyond description — not a technique's endpoint but what is said to remain when every stage before it is complete.
A map, not a method — the road is walked with a teacher, not a page.
Fabrizio Pregadio's translations and studies of neidan texts, including the Wuzhen pian tradition and the Zhong-Lü four/five-stage scheme.
