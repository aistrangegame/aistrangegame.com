Papaji (H.W.L. Poonja)

1910 – 1997 · A portrait

A householder from Lucknow met his teacher in 1944 and, decades later, began receiving Western seekers by the hundreds — many of whom left believing a single meeting had permanently awakened them.

Moment 1 of 5 · 1944

The meeting

At Tiruvannamalai in 1944, he sat before Ramana Maharshi expecting a vision of God. Instead the sage turned his attention inward, asking who it was that wanted to see God. He said he recognized the Self in that meeting and never doubted it again.

Moment 2 of 5 · to 1966

Ordinary years

He returned to householder life, working for decades to support an extended family, and did not retire from that work until 1966. He held no following and gave no public teaching during these years.

Moment 3 of 5 · 1990s

Arrivals

Through the 1990s, Western seekers began arriving in Lucknow in growing numbers, drawn by word of mouth to sit with him. He offered no ashram, no organization, and no teaching beyond pointing them back to themselves.

Moment 4 of 5 · 1993

Satsang

Visitors including the future teacher Mooji sat with him in the early 1990s. He taught, as the tradition holds it, that freedom needed no practice or path — only direct recognition of what was already true, here, now.

Moment 5 of 5 · 1997

The end

He died in Lucknow on 6 September 1997. Some of those close to him later wrote that many visitors’ glimpses faded within days — and how easily a glimpse was mistaken for lasting awakening.

A meeting opens something. Staying open takes longer.
Several of his Western visitors, including Gangaji, went on to teach in his name — and did not always agree, publicly or privately, about what they'd experienced.
