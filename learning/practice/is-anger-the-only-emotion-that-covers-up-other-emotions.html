Is Anger the Only Emotion That Covers Up Other Emotions?

A practice card

No. Anger is the one people notice most often doing this, but numbness, irritability, and even a forced calm can sit on top of something harder in the same way.

When
When a feeling seems to be masking something else — anger over a loss, irritability instead of fear, a calm that seems too composed for the situation.
What's actually happening
Anger gets singled out because it's loud and easy to spot, and because it can feel safer than the vulnerability underneath it, such as hurt, fear, or shame. That same function isn't unique to anger. Numbness can sit over grief, irritability over anxiety, and a forced calm over fear, whenever the feeling underneath seems harder to have than the one on top.
What to change
When a strong reaction shows up, ask what feeling would be there if this one weren't. That question works for any emotion, not only anger.
Watch for a reaction that seems bigger, or smaller, than the situation calls for. A mismatch is often a sign something else is underneath.
Consider whether the visible feeling is one that feels more acceptable than the one it might be sitting over. Fear and shame get covered often.
If you can find a feeling underneath, name both out loud — the one on top and the one under it. Neither cancels the other out.
Don't take it as given that a cover-up emotion is always anger. Numbness, irritability, and forced calm can do the same job.

What to expect
This isn't a rule that applies every time a feeling shows up — sometimes anger is just anger, with nothing underneath it. Pick one way of checking and use it for two weeks before deciding how often it turns out to be true for you.
