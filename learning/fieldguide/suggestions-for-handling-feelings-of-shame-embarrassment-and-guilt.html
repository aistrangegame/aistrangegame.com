Handling Shame, Guilt, and Embarrassment

A field guide

A lookup for the moment shame, guilt, or embarrassment hits — three different feelings, three different things that actually help.

Which is it?
Guilt is sitting on you — you did something wrongShame has you wanting to disappearSomething embarrassing just happened and your face is hot
1 of 3

When

Guilt is sitting on you — you did something wrong

Do

Name the specific thing, not the whole of you.
Decide one real amend you can make.
Make it soon, even if it's small.

Because

Guilt points at an action, and an action can usually be addressed; making the amends tends to loosen the knot faster than replaying it does.
2 of 3

When

Shame has you wanting to disappear

Do

Notice the words: are they about what you did, or who you are?
Say the shame thought out loud to yourself.
Ask: would I say this to someone I love?

Because

Shame says something is wrong with you, not with what you did, and no apology reaches that claim; questioning it tends to work better than obeying it.
3 of 3

When

Something embarrassing just happened and your face is hot

Do

Let the flush pass — it fades on its own.
Name it lightly if you can: that was awkward.
Do the next ordinary thing instead of replaying it.

Because

Embarrassment is mostly about being seen, and it tends to fade fastest of the three once attention moves on — it rarely means anything lasting about you.
Not every hard feeling is telling you the truth.
If shame keeps you stuck for weeks, not hours, that's worth taking to a therapist.
