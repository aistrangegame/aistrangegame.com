What exactly is Diwali and why is it called the "Festival of Lights"?
Diwali, derived from the Sanskrit word "Deepavali" meaning "row of lights," is one of the most significant festivals in Hindu tradition and among Indian communities worldwide. It's called the Festival of Lights because the central ritual involves lighting diyas (oil lamps) to symbolize the victory of light over darkness, knowledge over ignorance, and good over evil.

What are the core religious meanings behind Diwali celebrations?
Diwali carries multiple layers of religious significance across different traditions. For Hindus, it primarily commemorates three major mythological events: Lord Rama's victorious return to Ayodhya after 14 years of exile, Lord Krishna's defeat of the demon Narakasura, and the worship of Goddess Lakshmi for prosperity.

How has Diwali evolved from its ancient origins to modern celebrations?
Diwali's evolution spans millennia, beginning as an ancient harvest festival marking the end of the agricultural season in Vedic times. Originally focused on simple oil lamp lighting and gratitude for the harvest, it gradually incorporated elaborate mythological narratives and regional customs.

What are the essential rituals and practices that define Diwali observance?
Diwali observance centers around several key rituals performed over five days. The essential practices include thorough cleaning and decorating homes with rangoli (colorful floor patterns), lighting diyas and candles throughout the house, performing Lakshmi Puja (worship of the goddess of wealth), preparing and sharing traditional sweets, exchanging gifts with family and friends, and setting off fireworks.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
How do environmental concerns challenge traditional Diwali practices, and what tensions arise between cultural authenticity and ecological responsibility?
The environmental impact of Diwali, particularly from fireworks and increased consumption, creates significant tension between cultural tradition and ecological responsibility. Fireworks contribute to severe air and noise pollution, exacerbating health problems in already polluted urban areas.
Words to know
AyodhyaThe ancient city in northern India that serves as the setting for the Ramayana epic, where Lord Rama returned after his 14-year exile.
Bandi Chhor DivasThe Sikh festival that coincides with Diwali, commemorating the release of Guru Hargobind and 52 Hindu kings from Mughal imprisonment.
DeepavaliThe original Sanskrit name for Diwali, meaning "row of lights" or "series of lamps." Understanding this etymology is crucial because it directly connects to the festival's central practice of lighting oil lamps and explains why light symbolism is fundamental to all Diwali celebrations.
