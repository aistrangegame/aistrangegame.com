What are the Four Noble Truths and why are they considered the foundation of Buddhism?
The Four Noble Truths form Buddhism's diagnostic framework for understanding human existence and suffering. First, the Truth of Suffering (dukkha) recognizes that life inherently involves dissatisfaction, pain, and impermanence.

How does the Buddhist concept of "no-self" (anatta) differ from other religious understandings of the soul or self?
The Buddhist doctrine of anatta (no-self) stands in stark contrast to most religious traditions that posit an eternal, unchanging soul or self. While Hinduism teaches about the atman (eternal soul), Christianity speaks of an immortal soul, and Islam describes the ruh (spirit), Buddhism argues that what we consider "self" is actually a constantly changing collection of physical and mental processes called the Five Aggregates (form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness).
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What is the relationship between karma and rebirth in Buddhist teaching?
In Buddhism, karma and rebirth are intimately connected through the principle of ethical causality. Karma refers to intentional actions (physical, verbal, and mental) that create consequences extending beyond this lifetime.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
How has Buddhism evolved and adapted in modern times while maintaining its core identity?
Buddhism has undergone remarkable adaptation in the modern era while preserving its essential teachings. In the West, Buddhism has been integrated with psychology and neuroscience, leading to evidence-based mindfulness practices used in healthcare and education.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Is Buddhism truly a religion, or is it better understood as a philosophy or psychological system?
This question reveals the complexity of religious categorization and challenges Western definitions of religion. Buddhism exhibits characteristics of both religion and philosophy.
Words to know
Anatman (No-Self)The Buddhist doctrine that there is no permanent, unchanging self or soul within individuals.
ArhatIn Theravada Buddhism, an enlightened individual who has achieved liberation from the cycle of rebirth through personal effort.
Bodhi TreeThe sacred fig tree under which Siddhartha Gautama achieved enlightenment and became the Buddha.
More words
BodhisattvaIn Mahayana Buddhism, an enlightened being who postpones their own final liberation to help all sentient beings achieve enlightenment.
BuddhaLiterally meaning "awakened one," this title refers to Siddhartha Gautama, the historical founder of Buddhism, and represents anyone who has achieved complete enlightenment.
