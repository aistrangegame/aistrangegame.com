Sri Aurobindo

An icebreaker

A former revolutionary turned philosopher taught that evolution isn't finished — that a higher power of consciousness, the supermind, could descend into ordinary matter and transform it from within. His closest collaborator later declared that descent had already begun.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What is the supermind, according to this teaching?
As he taught it, the supermind is a higher power of consciousness above ordinary mind, capable of transforming matter itself rather than just perceiving it. He held it as the next real stage of evolution, not a metaphor.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
Who declared that this 'supramental descent' had happened?
His closest collaborator, who led the ashram's daily life, announced on 29 February 1956 that the supramental force had manifested on earth as a living fact. The claim rests entirely on her testimony.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What was this teacher's life before he became a spiritual teacher?
He was first a political revolutionary, jailed by British colonial authorities over a bombing conspiracy case and later acquitted. After his release he turned fully toward yoga and left politics behind.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
Has his life story faced any real scrutiny?
Yes — a later biographer who lived decades inside the ashram wrote a more skeptical account questioning claims of supernatural power. The book was formally banned in one Indian state after protests from devotees.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Is the practice still followed today?
Yes — a community built around this teaching still practices it at an ashram in southern India, and at a nearby international township founded specifically to embody it. Both remain active communities.

A voice · 1 of 4
Sri Aurobindo · Philosopher and former revolutionary

After early imprisonment for revolutionary activity, he withdrew from politics and spent decades developing a philosophy holding that a higher 'supermind' could descend and transform ordinary matter, not just consciousness. He set this out across dense philosophical works written mostly after 1910.

A voice · 2 of 4
Mirra Alfassa (The Mother) · His closest collaborator and successor

She ran the ashram's daily life for decades and, on 29 February 1956, announced that the supramental force had descended and become a living fact on earth. She described her own inner development as running the same course as his.

A voice · 3 of 4
Peter Heehs · Biographer who questioned the supernatural claims

Writing from inside the ashram archives, he argued that accepting the teacher as a divine incarnation is ultimately a matter of faith, not demonstrated fact. His 2008 biography was banned in one Indian state after devotees called it insulting.

A voice · 4 of 4
Satprem · Disciple who recorded her later conversations

As her secretary from 1957 onward, he recorded years of her private conversations about the supramental work, later publishing them as a multi-volume record. His earlier book remains a widely read introduction to the pair's teaching.

A line to leave them with
One man's philosophy, one woman's declaration — the descent itself stays a matter of faith.
