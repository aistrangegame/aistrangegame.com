Bernardo Kastrup

An icebreaker

A Dutch philosopher and computer scientist argues that reality is fundamentally mental, with individual minds as dissociated fragments of one universal consciousness. He holds this as a rigorous alternative to physicalism; other philosophers argue his central move doesn't actually solve what it claims to.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What is his core claim?
He argues consciousness is the one fundamental thing that exists, and that the physical world, including brains and individual people, are how one universal mind appears from the outside, a position he calls analytic idealism.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What is his background?
He holds a doctorate in computer engineering and one in philosophy, and worked as a scientist at research labs including CERN before turning to writing and directing a foundation devoted to consciousness studies.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
How does he explain individual minds?
He compares them to dissociated identities within one larger mind, similar to how dissociative identity disorder splits a single psyche into parts that don't share each other's experience, though he presents this as an analogy, not a diagnosis.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
What's the main philosophical objection to his view?
Critics, including a leading philosopher of panpsychism, argue his framework doesn't actually dissolve the hard problem of consciousness, it just moves the same explanatory gap to a different place, from how matter makes mind to how one mind divides into many.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Is this an accepted position in philosophy of mind?
No, it remains a minority view. He publishes peer-reviewed papers defending it and debates prominent physicalists and panpsychists directly, but most working philosophers of mind still hold that some form of physicalism is more likely true.
Words to know
Analytic idealismHis term for the claim that consciousness, not matter, is the base layer of reality.
Hard problem of consciousnessThe open question of why physical processes give rise to subjective experience at all.
CosmopsychismThe broader family of views holding that one universal consciousness is more fundamental than individual minds.
More words
Dissociation (as used here)His analogy for how one universal mind could appear to split into separate individual experiencers.
A voice · 1 of 3
Bernardo Kastrup · Philosopher who developed the theory

Across his peer-reviewed papers and books, he has argued that mind is the only thing known directly to exist, and that treating it as fundamental avoids problems he says plague physicalist and panpsychist accounts of consciousness alike.

A voice · 2 of 3
Philip Goff · Panpsychist philosopher who debated him

In public exchanges and essays responding directly to Kastrup, he argued for panpsychism over idealism, holding that basic physical entities themselves carry consciousness rather than being appearances of one larger cosmic mind.

A voice · 3 of 3
Keith Frankish · Philosopher who argues consciousness is illusory

Taking the illusionist position in exchanges with Kastrup, he maintained that the vivid, irreducible feel of experience Kastrup treats as bedrock is itself something a physical system can be built to represent, not proof mind is fundamental.
