Fritz-Albert Popp — Biophotons

An icebreaker

German biophysicist Fritz-Albert Popp built on an established fact: living cells emit extremely weak light. He argued that light was coherent and originated in DNA, carrying information between cells, an interpretation most biophysicists still dispute.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What is the accepted, measured fact here?
Since the 1920s, and confirmed by sensitive detectors from the 1950s onward, researchers have measured that living cells and tissues continuously emit extremely faint light, far too weak to see, now called ultraweak photon emission.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What did Popp add to that measurement?
In the 1970s he coined the term biophoton and argued the emission was coherent, like laser light, tracing its source to DNA and proposing it functioned as a signal coordinating activity between cells.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
Is the coherence claim accepted by mainstream science?
No. His measurements of faint light are not disputed, but physicists have found no accepted mechanism for coherence surviving in the warm, wet environment of a cell, and few have replicated his strongest claims.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
What's the leading alternative explanation for the light?
Many researchers attribute it to oxidative stress, chemical byproducts released as cells process oxygen during normal metabolism, treating the glow as a kind of cellular exhaust rather than a coordinated signal.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Where did this research area originate?
A Russian biologist reported something similar in 1923, naming it mitogenetic radiation after observing what looked like accelerated cell division nearby; interest collapsed after failed replications in the 1930s, decades before the biophoton research began.
Words to know
BiophotonPopp's term for the ultraweak light continuously emitted by living cells and tissues.
Ultraweak photon emissionThe measured phenomenon itself: extremely low-intensity light given off by metabolizing cells, detectable only with sensitive instruments.
CoherenceA property of light where waves stay in a fixed, laser-like relationship, unlike ordinary scattered light.
More words
Oxidative stressChemical byproducts of reactive oxygen produced during normal metabolism, a leading alternative explanation for the emitted light.
Mitogenetic radiationAn earlier, largely abandoned 1920s claim that cells emit radiation that speeds nearby cell division.
A voice · 1 of 3
Fritz-Albert Popp · German biophysicist who coined 'biophoton'

From the 1970s he argued that ultraweak light from cells was coherent and originated in DNA, proposing in his research that it functioned as a signal coordinating activity across an organism.

A voice · 2 of 3
Alexander Gurwitsch · Russian biologist, earlier discoverer of the effect

In 1923 he reported that dividing onion root cells seemed to accelerate division in nearby cells, naming the effect mitogenetic radiation; the finding drew wide interest before failing replication in the 1930s.

A voice · 3 of 3
Michal Cifra · Biophysicist who co-authored a critical review

In a 2015 review written with colleagues, he concluded that ultraweak photon emission from living cells is experimentally well established, but found no reliable evidence to date for the coherence Popp claimed.

A line to leave them with
The glow is real. What it means for the cell is still argued.
