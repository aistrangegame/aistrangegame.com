What exactly is Kundalini, and why is it described as a "coiled serpent"?
Kundalini is a Sanskrit term meaning "coiled one" or "serpent power," referring to a form of dormant spiritual energy believed to reside at the base of the spine. It's depicted as a coiled serpent because this energy is thought to lie in a dormant, coiled state until awakened.

How does the chakra system relate to Kundalini awakening, and what are the nadis?
The chakra system provides the energetic framework through which Kundalini energy travels during awakening. There are seven main chakras (energy centers) located along the spine, each associated with different aspects of consciousness and human experience.

What's the difference between spontaneous and induced Kundalini awakening?
Spontaneous Kundalini awakening occurs unexpectedly, often triggered by intense emotional experiences, trauma, spiritual practices, or even physical events. This type of awakening can be overwhelming because the individual hasn't prepared their body and mind for the energy shift.

What are the fundamental principles underlying how Kundalini energy moves through the body?
Kundalini operates on the principle of energy rising from the base of the spine through progressively higher consciousness centers. The energy naturally seeks to ascend through the Sushumna nadi (central channel), but blockages in the chakras or energy channels can impede this flow.

Are there significant risks to Kundalini awakening that challenge the common portrayal of it as purely beneficial?
Yes, Kundalini awakening presents substantial risks that are often underemphasized in popular presentations. The research notes clearly state that while awakening can lead to profound spiritual growth, it "also presents risks if not managed properly" and should be approached "with caution and under proper guidance." Risks include intense emotional releases that can destabilize daily functioning, physical symptoms like energy surges or nervous system overwhelm, and psychological effects ranging from euphoria to confusion or even temporary psychosis-like states.
