The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis

An icebreaker

A group of researchers argues that fragments of a disintegrating comet struck or exploded over the Earth around 12,900 years ago, triggering sudden cold and mass extinctions. Other scientists who reviewed the same evidence say it doesn't hold together as a real impact.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What exactly does the hypothesis propose?
It proposes that around 12,900 years ago, fragments from a disintegrating comet struck or exploded above the Earth, triggering wildfires, a sudden return to near-glacial cold, and the extinction of large ice-age animals across parts of the globe.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
Who first proposed it and when?
A physicist and a group of collaborators, including a marine geologist, formally introduced the hypothesis in a 2007 paper, pointing to unusual materials found in sediment layers from that period across multiple continents.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What evidence do proponents point to?
They cite tiny magnetic spherules, nanodiamonds, and elevated platinum in soil layers dated to the period, materials they argue are signatures of a cosmic impact rather than anything produced by ordinary Earth processes.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
What's the strongest objection critics raise?
A 2023 review led by an archaeologist called the case scientifically unconvincing, noting no crater from the period has ever been found and that the proposed evidence hasn't converged into one coherent, reproducible scenario.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Is this settled either way?
No. Proponents published a detailed rebuttal defending their evidence as reproduced at dozens of sites worldwide, and the exchange between the two camps has continued through several rounds of dueling papers without resolution.
Words to know
Younger DryasA sudden return to near-glacial cold beginning about 12,900 years ago, whose cause is still disputed.
Nanospherule / nanodiamondMicroscopic particles cited as possible signatures of extreme heat or pressure from a cosmic impact.
Platinum group anomalyAn unusually high level of platinum-family elements in a soil layer, rare on Earth but common in comets and asteroids.
More words
Clovis cultureAn early North American toolmaking tradition whose decline around this period is part of the disputed timeline.
A voice · 1 of 3
Richard Firestone · Physicist who co-led the 2007 proposal

Publishing with a team of collaborators in 2007, he argued that magnetic and carbon-rich material found in sediment layers across North America pointed to a cosmic impact or airburst around 12,900 years ago.

A voice · 2 of 3
James Kennett · Geologist and co-author of the hypothesis

Building on the original 2007 paper, he has continued to argue the impact evidence found at dozens of sites worldwide is reproducible, and that dismissing the hypothesis without addressing that body of data is premature.

A voice · 3 of 3
Vance Holliday · Archaeologist who led a 2023 review

Reviewing the evidence in detail with colleagues, he concluded the hypothesis had not converged into a self-consistent scenario, noting the absence of any dated impact crater from the period as a central unresolved problem.
