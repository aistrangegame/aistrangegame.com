Graham Hancock

An icebreaker

A journalist argues an advanced ice-age civilization was destroyed by a comet-driven cataclysm and its survivors seeded ancient cultures worldwide. Archaeologists say the sites he cites are well explained by known history, and no physical trace of that civilization has ever been found.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What is his central claim?
He argues a globally advanced civilization existed during the last ice age and was wiped out by comet impacts around 12,000 years ago, with a few survivors then teaching farming and monument-building to hunter-gatherers worldwide.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What is his professional background?
He worked for years as a journalist, including as East Africa correspondent for a major newspaper, before turning to books on ancient mysteries starting with a bestseller in 1995. He has no formal training in archaeology.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What's the main archaeological objection to his theory?
Archaeologists point out that an advanced civilization would leave the same traces every other culture does — crops, tools, waste, buildings — and none turn up anywhere from that period. They call the theory unsupported by the actual record.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
Did he debate a working archaeologist directly?
In a 2024 podcast debate lasting over four hours, an archaeologist walked through the excavated evidence site by site. The author has continued to make the same case since.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Has this reached a wide audience?
Yes — his books have sold in the millions, and a Netflix docuseries built around his ideas ran for two seasons, drawing far more viewers than most academic archaeology ever reaches.
Words to know
DiffusionismThe idea that major human advances spread from one original source rather than developing independently in many places.
Younger DryasA sudden return to near-glacial cold beginning about 12,900 years ago, whose cause is still debated.
PseudoarchaeologyClaims presented in the style of archaeology without meeting its standards of evidence, peer review, or testing.
More words
Comet-impact hypothesisThe proposed but unproven idea that fragments from a disintegrating comet struck Earth around 12,900 years ago.
A voice · 1 of 3
Graham Hancock · Journalist who argues for a lost civilization

Beginning with a 1995 bestseller, he has argued that survivors of an advanced ice-age civilization, destroyed by comet-driven catastrophe, seeded farming and monument-building traditions across separate ancient cultures worldwide.

A voice · 2 of 3
John Hoopes · Anthropologist who has studied his claims for decades

Having followed his writing for more than thirty years, he argues Hancock's work revives old diffusionist ideas that assume advanced knowledge must have spread from one source, a framework mainstream archaeology abandoned for lack of evidence.

A voice · 3 of 3
Flint Dibble · Archaeologist who debated him publicly

In an April 2024 podcast debate lasting over four hours, he walked through excavated evidence site by site, arguing an advanced civilization would have left ordinary traces — crops, tools, waste — that simply don't exist in the record.

A line to leave them with
The sites are real and excavated. What built them is not settled by a bestseller.
