Ingo Swann

An icebreaker

An American artist who described lifelong experiences of perceiving distant places became central to a government-funded research protocol, coining the term 'remote viewing' in 1971. Skeptics found cues in the early SRI experiments that could explain results without anything paranormal.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
Who was he before this research began?
Born in 1933, he was a working artist in New York who had described unusual perceptual experiences for much of his life before approaching parapsychology researchers in the early 1970s to have those claims formally tested.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What did he actually contribute?
Working with physicists at the Stanford Research Institute starting in 1972, he coined the term 'remote viewing' and helped design a structured protocol meant to separate a viewer's raw impressions from their own guessing or analysis.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What is coordinate remote viewing?
Later in his SRI work, he developed a more structured method in which a viewer was given only geographic coordinates as a target reference, a technique that became the basis for the government's later, more formal training program.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
What's a specific claim tied to him?
During a 1973 session, he described Jupiter as having a ring system years before that was confirmed by the Voyager space probe in 1979, an episode his supporters cite and skeptics attribute to guesswork or existing scientific speculation at the time.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
How did skeptical reviewers assess the early SRI work?
A 1978 critique by two psychologists argued that transcripts from the early SRI remote-viewing experiments contained cues, like dates or remarks, that could let judges match descriptions to targets without any anomalous perception.
Words to know
Remote viewingThe claimed ability to describe a distant or hidden location using only a coded reference, a term he coined in 1971.
Coordinate remote viewing (CRV)A structured protocol he developed using geographic coordinates as the only target reference given to a viewer.
CueingUnconscious hints in a research setup, such as tone or phrasing, that critics argue could explain results without paranormal perception.

SRIThe Stanford Research Institute, where he worked with physicists on remote-viewing research beginning in 1972.
A voice · 1 of 3
Ingo Swann · Artist who developed the protocol

Working with SRI researchers from 1972 onward, he maintained that his perceptual experiences reflected a trainable skill rather than a rare gift, and helped design staged methods meant to let ordinary people be trained to describe hidden targets.

A voice · 2 of 3
Harold Puthoff · Physicist who worked with him at SRI

Co-leading the research program he joined, Puthoff credited Swann's early sessions and design input as central to developing the structured protocol the lab used for its remote-viewing experiments through the 1970s and 1980s.

A voice · 3 of 3
David Marks · Psychologist who reviewed SRI transcripts

Publishing a critique with a colleague in 1978, he argued that transcripts from the early SRI remote-viewing experiments contained cues that could let judges match descriptions to targets, accounting for apparent accuracy without anything paranormal.
