Helena Blavatsky

An icebreaker

A Russian-born occultist published a 1,500-page cosmology claiming ancient masters had shown her humanity's real history, told in seven root races across lost continents. A Society for Psychical Research investigator called her letters from those masters forgeries; a later re-examination called his report unfair.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What does the book actually claim?
Published in 1888, it presents itself as a commentary on ancient stanzas she said were shown to her by hidden teachers, tracing cosmic and human evolution through seven stages and root races on continents said to be lost, including Atlantis.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What did the 1885 investigation find?
A Society for Psychical Research investigator sent to India examined letters she said came from her hidden teachers and concluded in a 1885 report that she had written them herself and staged the phenomena around them.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
Was that investigation itself above criticism?
A century later, a document examiner and longtime society member re-examined the report and called it slanted, full of conjecture presented as fact and testimony from unnamed witnesses. He did not conclude the letters were genuine.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
Did she copy from other writers?
A critic who compared her text with earlier books identified long unattributed passages drawn from other authors' works on mythology and religion, woven into her own larger synthesis.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
What did she actually start?
In 1875 she co-founded the Theosophical Society, which spread her ideas about hidden wisdom behind world religions and shaped later New Age and esoteric movements well beyond her own writings.
Words to know
TheosophyHer movement's claim that a single hidden wisdom underlies all the world's religions.

Mahatma lettersLetters she said were dictated or delivered by hidden spiritual teachers, later at the center of the fraud investigation.

A voice · 1 of 4
Helena Blavatsky · Co-founder of the Theosophical Society

In her 1888 book she claimed to be transmitting an ancient cosmology shown to her by hidden teachers, describing human history as a series of root races unfolding across vast stretches of time and several lost continents.

A voice · 2 of 4
Richard Hodgson · Investigator for the Society for Psychical Research

In his December 1885 report he concluded that letters attributed to her hidden teachers were frauds that she had written herself, and named her one of the most accomplished impostors of the era.

A voice · 3 of 4
Vernon Harrison · Document examiner who re-examined the case

In a 1986 review he found the 1885 report riddled with slanted statements, conjecture presented as fact, and uncorroborated testimony, though he did not conclude the disputed letters were genuinely from her claimed teachers.

A voice · 4 of 4
William Emmette Coleman · Critic who traced her sources

In an 1895 published analysis he identified specific earlier books on mythology, religion, and science from which he argued she drew extensive unattributed material.

A line to leave them with
The teachers may be legend. What people built from her words was not.
