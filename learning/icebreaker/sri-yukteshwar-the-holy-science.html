Sri Yukteshwar — The Holy Science

An icebreaker

A Bengali swami published a 1894 book arguing that history moves in cycles of roughly 24,000 years, driven by the sun orbiting a companion star, and that humanity is now rising rather than declining. Modern astronomy measures that cycle differently.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What book is this, and when was it written?
A Bengali swami wrote 'The Holy Science' in 1894, aiming to show that the Bible and the Hindu Upanishads point to the same underlying truth, laid out through parallel passages from both traditions.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
What is his yuga-cycle claim?
As he held it, the traditional Hindu ages, or yugas, don't span millions of years as most pandits of his time taught. He proposed a roughly 24,000-year cycle instead, and argued humanity had already left the low point and entered a rising age.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What did he say causes this cycle?
He proposed that the sun orbits a companion star roughly every 24,000 years, and that this orbit, not just Earth's own wobble, drives the slow drift of the equinoxes he used to date the ages.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
What does mainstream astronomy say about that mechanism?
Astronomers measure the equinoxes' drift as a wobble of Earth's own axis, a cycle of about 25,800 years, with no observational evidence for a solar companion star driving it. His binary-star explanation isn't accepted by astronomers.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
How is the book read today?
Within the yoga lineage that produced it, the book is held as a teacher's reasoned attempt to date the ages by natural law rather than tradition alone. Its precession figure and companion-star mechanism remain his own claim, not a verified finding.
Words to know
YugaOne of four ages in Hindu cosmology, traditionally cycling from a golden age down to a dark age and back.
Precession of the equinoxesThe slow, roughly 25,800-year wobble of Earth's axis, which shifts where the equinox points fall against the stars.
Dwapara YugaThe third of the four yugas in the traditional order; he argued the world entered it in the 1800s, not the lower Kali Yuga.
More words
Kaivalya DarsanamThe book's original Sanskrit title, meaning roughly 'the vision of oneness,' before English editions used 'The Holy Science.'
A voice · 1 of 3
Sri Yukteshwar · Author of the 1894 book

He argued that the equinoxes' precession traces the sun's own roughly 24,000-year orbit around a companion star, and used that figure to recalculate the yuga ages, concluding humanity had already risen out of the lowest age.

A voice · 2 of 3
Paramahansa Yogananda · His student who publicized the book's ideas

In his 1946 autobiography, he described this as his teacher's own reasoned correction of the traditional yuga calendar, presenting the 24,000-year cycle as a serious contribution rather than settled science.

A voice · 3 of 3
Philip Goldberg · Biographer who researched Yogananda and his lineage

In his 2018 biography of Yogananda, he traced the teacher-student relationship with Yukteshwar and the book's role in it, presenting the 1894 work as a document of that lineage rather than treating its cosmology as established fact.
