Celtic / Druid Tradition

An icebreaker

Roman writers described druids as judges, teachers, and priests among the ancient Celts, though modern historians doubt how much those outsiders truly understood. Since the 1700s, a separate revival movement has built its own Druidry from scratch, openly modern and centered on nature.

Worth knowing · 1 of 5
What do the ancient sources actually say about druids?
Roman writers described druids among the Gauls and Britons as judges, teachers, and religious specialists who oversaw sacrifice and settled disputes, along with claims of human sacrifice that modern historians treat with real caution.
Worth knowing · 2 of 5
Why is that evidence treated cautiously?
The main accounts come from Roman conquerors describing a people they were actively subjugating, and historians note the writers may have shaped druids to resemble Roman priesthoods or to justify Rome's conquest.
Worth knowing · 3 of 5
What does archaeology add to the picture?
Sites with mass burials and a preserved bog body showing signs of ritual killing suggest sacrificial practices existed in Iron Age Britain and Gaul, though linking any specific find directly to druids remains uncertain.
Worth knowing · 4 of 5
When did modern Druidry begin?
A revival took shape in 18th-century Britain, driven by antiquarians and Romantic-era writers who reimagined druids as wise nature priests, building new orders and ceremonies with only a loose connection to ancient sources.
Worth knowing · 5 of 5
Is modern Druidry the same as the ancient religion?
No. As modern Druid organizations describe their own practice, it is an openly contemporary, nature-centered spirituality built during the revival, not a continuous transmission from Iron Age priests, and its members generally say so themselves.
Words to know
DruidIn Roman-era sources, a priestly and judicial class among Iron Age Celtic peoples in Gaul and Britain, known to us mainly through outsider accounts.
Druid revivalThe reinvention of druidry as a modern movement beginning in 18th-century Britain, drawing loosely on classical sources and Romantic imagination.
Iolo MorganwgAn 18th-century Welsh writer whose invented rituals and texts, later shown to be largely his own creation, deeply shaped modern Druid practice.
More words
Modern DruidryContemporary Druid orders practicing nature-centered spirituality today, generally understood by their own members as a modern tradition, not an unbroken ancient one.
A voice · 1 of 4
Julius Caesar · Roman general who wrote on Gaulish druids

In his account of the Gallic wars, he described druids as overseeing sacrifice, teaching, and judging disputes among the Gauls, a portrait modern historians read as shaped partly by Rome's own political interests.

A voice · 2 of 4
Pliny the Elder · Roman writer who described druid ritual

In his natural history, he described druids gathering mistletoe with a golden sickle in a ceremony honoring the plant's sacred status, one of the few surviving details of druidic ritual practice from antiquity.

A voice · 3 of 4
Ronald Hutton · Historian of British paganism and druidry

In his history of the druids in Britain, he traced how little is reliably known of the ancient priesthood and how thoroughly the 18th-century revival, especially through one influential Welsh forger, reinvented druidry as something new.

A voice · 4 of 4
Philip Carr-Gomm · Leader of a modern Druid order

Reviving and expanding a Druid teaching order from the late 1980s onward, he has presented modern Druidry as a nature-centered spiritual path for the present, openly distinct from any claim of unbroken ancient descent.
