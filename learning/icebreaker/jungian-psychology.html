Who was Carl Jung and how did his background shape his psychological theories?
Carl Gustav Jung (1875-1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst whose unique background profoundly influenced his psychological theories. Born in Switzerland, Jung initially collaborated with Sigmund Freud but later broke away due to fundamental theoretical differences.

What are the core components of the psyche according to Jung, and how do they interact?
Jung structured the psyche into several interconnected layers. The **ego** represents our conscious identity and everyday awareness.

What is individuation and why is it central to Jungian psychology?
Individuation is Jung's term for the lifelong process of becoming one's authentic self by integrating unconscious elements into conscious awareness. Unlike simple self-improvement, individuation involves confronting and assimilating rejected aspects of personality (the Shadow), balancing opposing psychological tendencies, and developing a relationship with the deeper Self beyond the ego.

How do Jung's psychological types help us understand individual differences?
Jung identified fundamental differences in how people perceive and process information through his theory of psychological types. He described four functions of consciousness: **thinking** (logical analysis), **feeling** (value-based decisions), **sensation** (concrete, present-moment awareness), and **intuition** (pattern recognition and future possibilities).

How does Jung's concept of synchronicity challenge conventional understanding of causality and meaning-making?
Synchronicity—Jung's term for meaningful coincidences—suggests that events can be connected by meaning rather than just cause-and-effect relationships. This challenges the purely materialistic worldview that dominates Western science by proposing that psychological states and external events can correspond in meaningful ways without one causing the other.
