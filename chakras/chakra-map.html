The first tree
Energy
Go up ↑ · Stillness

Your body has an energy system. Seven centers, running from the base of your spine to the top of your head. Each one governs a different frequency of your life — survival, desire, will, love, expression, perception, connection.

You've probably heard of chakras. This is what they actually are.

This system runs on stillness. The quieter you get, the more it moves.

Sahasrara
Crown
Connection. Not to something above you — to everything that includes you.
Be in nature. Not hiking — being. Sit under a tree. Watch clouds. Let the scale register.
Top of the head. The crown point. The soft spot that was open when you were an infant.
Governs connection to something larger than the individual self. The moment you feel awe — looking at stars, holding a newborn, standing at the edge of an ocean — that's the crown.

Sahasrara is the body's answer to the question: Am I alone? Not socially — existentially. Is there something beyond my individual experience? The crown doesn't answer this with philosophy. It answers with direct experience. You feel it or you don't. And most people have — at least once.

The crown operates on surrender. Every other chakra has a practice — something you do. The crown asks you to stop doing. When the root is grounded, the sacral is flowing, the solar plexus is burning cleanly, the heart is open, the throat is speaking, and the third eye is seeing — the crown opens on its own. You don't activate it. You allow it.

This is the quietest center. It doesn't announce itself. Sometimes it's just a moment of peace so complete that the boundary between you and everything else becomes transparent. You're still you. But you're also everything.

When this center is blocked, you feel isolated at a level that social connection can't touch. Not lonely for people — lonely for meaning. When it's open, meaning doesn't need to be found. It's already present. Everywhere.

Silence. Not meditation with a technique — just silence. Sit for five minutes with no input. The mind will resist. Let it resist. The crown is underneath the resistance, waiting.

The simplest practice: Gratitude. Not forced — genuine recognition of something that exists without your effort. Awe is the crown's natural frequency.

"The disease that was removed was the belief that you were less than what you are."
Golden — Huntrix · The Bindu learns to Shine Receive · Panel 7 Radiance
Ajna
Third Eye
Inner sight. The ability to see without using your eyes.
Close your eyes and sit in the dark. Let your eyes relax behind closed lids. Notice what appears. Just watch.
Between the eyebrows. Slightly behind the forehead, where the brain sits behind the brow ridge.
Governs intuition, perception, imagination, and the ability to see patterns and truths that aren't visible on the surface. You use this center every time you "see" a solution before you can explain it.

Ajna is the body's answer to the question: What do I see when I stop looking? Looking is active — scanning, searching. Seeing is receptive — what arrives when the scanning stops.

This center operates on a paradox: the harder you try to see, the less you perceive. The answer that arrives in the shower. The face of someone you were about to call when they call you. That's not magic. It's a perceptual system you were never taught to use deliberately.

Before making a decision, close your eyes and ask the question. Don't think about the answer — wait for it. The first image or sense that arrives is Ajna responding. It's faster than thought and less verbal.

"You stopped looking and the seeing arrived on its own."
Experience — Einaudi · The Bindu learns to See Still · Panel 6 Vision
Vishuddha
Throat
Your voice. Not the sound — the truth behind it.
Sing. In the shower, in the car. Not performing — vibrating. The vocal cords are strings.
The throat. The narrowest passage in the body — everything that rises must pass through here.
Governs expression, truth-telling, and the ability to say what you mean. The throat is a bottleneck by design. What gets through is what you choose to say. What gets blocked is what you're afraid to.

Vishuddha is the body's answer to the question: Will I say what I know? There's a difference between talking and speaking. Talking fills space. Speaking changes it.

When this center is blocked, you swallow your words. You say "fine" when you mean "not fine." Over time, the swallowed words become tension in the jaw, tightness in the neck. When it's open, you don't speak louder. You speak clearer.

Say something true out loud to an empty room. Something you've been thinking but haven't said. The throat doesn't care if anyone else is listening. It needs YOU to hear it.

Advanced practice: The next time someone asks how you are, don't say "good." Say something true. Even small truth opens this center.

"What you finally said out loud was what you always knew."
The Sound of Silence — Disturbed · The Bindu learns to Roar Speak · Panel 5 Truth
Anahata
Heart
The center where love lives. Not romantic love. The kind that doesn't need a reason.
Put your hand on your chest and breathe. Feel your heartbeat through your palm. Two minutes.
Center of the chest. Not the physical heart — the energetic heart, directly behind the sternum.
Governs love, compassion, grief, and the ability to give and receive without keeping score. Three below (physical), three above (spiritual), and the heart where they meet.

Anahata is the body's answer to the question: Can I love without protecting myself? Every wall around your heart is a reasonable response to something that hurt you. The heart doesn't ask you to tear the walls down. It asks: what if the walls were doors?

The Sanskrit name means "unstruck sound" — a sound that plays without anything being hit. The heart's frequency doesn't need a trigger. It's already playing. When you feel sudden love for no reason — walking down the street, looking at a stranger, watching light move through a window — that's Anahata. It was always playing. You just got quiet enough to hear it.

When this center is blocked, you protect. You give with conditions. You receive with suspicion. When it's open, love moves through you like breathing — not because you decided to be loving, but because you stopped deciding not to be.

Put your hand on your chest and breathe. That's it. Feel your heartbeat through your palm. Do this for two minutes. The heart responds to attention the way a plant responds to water.

Think of someone you love without complication — a child, a pet, a grandparent. Feel what happens in your chest. That warmth, that expansion, that softening. That's the heart center. It was already open. You just pointed at it.

Harder practice: Think of someone you're angry with. Feel the contraction in the chest. Now breathe into the contraction without trying to fix it. The heart doesn't need you to forgive. It needs you to stop holding your breath.

"The love that arrived for others was always your own heart opening."
Love Me Like You Do — Ellie Goulding · The Bindu learns to Reach Open · Panel 4 Recognition
Manipura
Solar Plexus
Your fire. The center that transforms what enters you into what you become.
Breath of Fire — rapid rhythmic breathing through the nose, pumping the navel. 30 seconds. You'll feel it immediately.
Upper abdomen, between the navel and the base of the ribcage. Named for the sun because it radiates.
Governs will, personal power, digestion (physical and psychological), and transformation. This is the furnace. What you eat, what you experience — Manipura is where it gets processed and turned into YOU.

Manipura is the body's answer to the question: What am I willing to do about it? It's the bridge between wanting (sacral) and acting. Will lives here. Not willpower — that's the mind forcing the body. Will is the fire that moves on its own when it has fuel.

When this center is blocked, you feel powerless. When overactive, you burn too hot — anger, control. The balanced state is steady fire: you know what you can do, you know what you will do, and there's no gap between them.

Core engagement — not crunches, but standing twists, planks, any movement that requires your center to stabilize the rest of you. Eat slowly and feel your digestion actually working.

"The fire that agitated you was building your form."
Powerful — Major Lazer · The Bindu learns to Burn Build · Panel 3 Contact
Svadhisthana
Sacral
Where desire lives. Not what you want — the fact that you want at all.
Move your hips. Dance without choreography. Allow yourself to want something without evaluating whether you should.
Lower abdomen, just below the navel. The space between the hip bones.
Governs creativity, desire, emotion, and pleasure. The center of flow — water energy, movement, the willingness to feel something and follow it without knowing where it leads.

Svadhisthana is the body's answer to the question: What do I want? Not the filtered, reasonable version. The raw one. The desire before the justification.

When it's open, you create freely. When it's blocked, desire either disappears or distorts into craving. The mechanism is water. Water doesn't push through obstacles — it flows around them. Stop forcing. Follow what feels alive.

Start making something without a plan. Draw without deciding what to draw. Write without knowing the ending. Hum a melody that doesn't exist yet. The sacral activates when you create without controlling.

"Every desire was drawing you toward yourself."
Crystallize — Lindsey Stirling · The Bindu learns to Flow Follow · Panel 2 Sensation
Muladhara
Root
The ground you stand on. Literally and in every other sense.
Walk barefoot on grass or earth. Stand still and feel the weight of your body pressing into the floor. Ten breaths.
Base of the spine. The pelvic floor. The lowest point of the torso.
Governs survival, safety, and your relationship with the physical world. When this center is active, you feel grounded. When it's blocked, everything feels unstable — not because the world is, but because your connection to it is.

Muladhara is the body's answer to the question: Am I safe here? It processes the signals from your environment — physical space, financial stability, the literal ground under your feet — and translates them into a felt sense of security or threat. It doesn't think about safety. It knows.

The root operates below conscious thought. You can't talk yourself into feeling grounded. You can only create the conditions — physical stability, reliable structure, contact with the earth — and let the root respond. It was here before language.

Sit on the ground. Garden. Cook with your hands in raw ingredients. Anything that puts your body in direct contact with physical matter.

Breath practice: Inhale through the nose, 4 counts. Hold, 4 counts. Exhale through the mouth with a low hum, 8 counts. The hum vibrates the pelvic floor. That vibration is the root waking up.

"The earth that holds you was chosen by you."
