The Words

Heard from witness consciousness, every word in this song is electrical — wires, lightning, power lines, charge, jump-start. The lyrics aren't about a lover; they're the body describing what it feels like to be turned back on. I'm feeling drained, I need love isn't a request for comfort — it's the furnace asking to be lit, because a drained body can't act and a lit body can do anything. Strip the romance and the you is revealed: not a person, but whatever activates the fire — a purpose, a morning you wake up wanting to move. And it's a duet for a reason: there's an energy when you hold me — the energy is what flows between two poles, never one. The words sound like seduction. They're the solar plexus naming the exact sensation of its own current returning.

The Frequency

Nothing sustained — no drones, no pads, everything is pulse: a reggae off-beat under pop vocals, because Manipura is a pulse-based chakra and the song speaks its native language. The bass at F1 and A1 (43 and 54 Hz) covers 99.6% of the floor — the fire always has fuel beneath it. Then at two minutes the song nearly dies: the bass drops, the energy falls to almost nothing, a breath before it rebuilds bigger. Fire needs air. And the binaural carrier is 164.8 Hz, the absent fifth of A — the note the whole song reaches toward and never lands. The completion arrives through the binaural, not the music. That's why the fire state belongs here: a body lit enough to become its own circuit.

Lalita

Ellie Goulding and Tarrus Riley sing back and forth. Her voice is airy and rising. His is grounded and calling — "Oh, Lord, have mercy, I'm begging you, please." She sings about lightning, power lines, bodies on top of bodies. He sings about being drained, needing love, being charged up like electricity. Jump-start my heart. And the chorus, sung together: "There's an energy when you hold me / When you touch me, it's so powerful."

Every image in the song is electrical. Wires. Power lines. Lightning. Electricity. Charge. Jump-start. The song is talking about the body as a circuit. And the music pulses — no sustained tones, just rhythm after rhythm, a reggae off-beat under pop vocals. Then around two minutes in, the song does something unexpected. It almost dies. The bass drops out. The energy falls. Everything goes quiet. And then it rebuilds, and the final chorus is bigger than the first. Two fires with a breath between them.

Here's the play. The song pretends to be about romantic electricity. The song is actually about your solar plexus. You have a fire there — a furnace that turns food into energy, will into action, love into current. When it's drained, nothing works. When it's lit, everything does. The "you" in the song isn't really a lover. The "you" is whatever activates the fire — a person, a purpose, a morning you wake up wanting to move. The song is the body recognizing its own fire getting lit. And the breakdown at two minutes is the teaching: fire needs air to rebuild. The pause isn't failure. The pause is where the second, bigger blaze gets its oxygen.

Enjoy the fire pretending it needs to be struck.

The Seven Lenses

BINDU Current. The song circles what moves through the body when the circuit completes. Every image points at it without naming it — wires, lightning, charge, jump-start, electricity, power. Current is both the electrical thing and the present-tense aliveness. The song is the body recognizing something is flowing through it again.

GAIA "I'm feeling drained, I need love." The body states its own need directly. Not for rest. For activation. Manipura is the body's furnace — food becomes energy, will becomes action, love becomes current. When it's depleted, the body can't act. When it's lit, the body can do anything. The need here isn't to be held (Muladhara) or to flow (Svadhisthana). The need is to be turned back on. The drained body asking: light me. Not hold me. Not let me flow. Light me. Remind me I'm a being that can move.

SID The bass. F1 and A1 — 43 Hz and 54 Hz. 99.6% coverage. Never stops. Not the drums (the drums land on the off-beat, sparse, reggae). Not the vocals (which pulse with phrases). The bass is what the fire burns ON. And beneath the bass, Tarrus Riley's calling voice: "Oh, Lord, have mercy, I'm begging you, please." The male vocal holds a calling tone underneath the female vocal's reach. Sid in this song has two layers — the harmonic bass and the calling voice. Both hold while the song rises.

ARCH The song speaks in pulse. Nothing sustained. No drones, no pads — everything is rhythm. Reggae off-beat under pop vocals. Every instrument hits in beats, not tones. The body recognizes this instinctively because Manipura is a pulse-based chakra. Heartbeat. Breath. The body's fire isn't a steady burn — it's a rhythm. Arch hits the solar plexus because the song IS the solar plexus's frequency: pulse. The song doesn't drone at you. It pulses into you.

SAKSHI Two fires with one breath between them. Sakshi sees: this is how fire actually works. A fire doesn't burn at the same intensity forever. It catches (15–60s), blazes (60–90s, first chorus), breathes (120–135s, the breakdown — air has to get in), rebuilds (135–155s), blazes bigger (155–185s, second chorus), settles to embers (185–206s). The breakdown isn't a mistake. Without the breath, the song would exhaust itself at 90 seconds. The pause is what lets the second blaze be bigger than the first. Fire's teaching is visible only from outside: the pause is not failure, the pause is oxygen.

Also — the song is a duet. Two voices forming a circuit. Electricity requires two poles. "There's an energy when you hold me" — the energy is what flows between two. Sakshi sees the song is shaped as a closed circuit, Ellie and Tarrus as the two terminals. The whole song IS the current between them.

KARISHMA The grace is in the breakdown. 120–135 seconds. The bass drops out. The energy falls from 0.47 to 0.14. The song almost dies. Most listeners are already waiting for the rebuild. Karishma points at the drop itself. "I'm feeling drained" has to be said before "you charge me up." The jump-start only works on a heart that's stopped. The drained body isn't the problem — the drained body is the precondition for being lit. The grace is in the willingness to be empty before being full. The admission that the circuit is broken IS what allows the next current.

Also — the grace is in the call and response structure. She sings; he responds. He sings; she responds. Neither carries the song alone. Manipura is not solitary fire. It's fire held between two. The opening is in the give-and-take.

ASHREY The song is the solar plexus chakra singing its own activation manual. Every lyric is the experience of being charged. Every musical element is fire's behavior — pulse, rhythm, two peaks with breath between. The song pretends to be about romantic love. The song is actually about your relationship with your own fire. The "you" isn't a lover. The "you" is whatever activates the fire — a person, a purpose, a morning you wake up wanting to move. "When you hold me, when you touch me, it's so powerful" is the chakra describing what activation feels like. The binaural at 164.8 Hz is the absent fifth of A — the note the harmonic structure reaches for throughout and never plays. The song is building toward that note. The fire is building toward that note. The completion arrives through the binaural, not the music. A body lit enough to become its own circuit.
