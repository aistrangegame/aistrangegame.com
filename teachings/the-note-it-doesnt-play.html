The Note It Doesn’t Play

A thing is defined by what it reaches for and never says

The teaching
The most honest way to understand something is to find what it keeps reaching toward without ever stating — and put that underneath it.
Before the laws
Under every song in a certain music app there's a low tone you can't quite hear. It isn't the song's key. It's the note the song leans toward and never plays — the absent fifth, the ground it was standing on. A machine, asked where a song peaks, finds the loudest moment. A person listening finds the meaningful one, twenty-two seconds later. That gap is the whole craft.
1 of 4
Look for the absence, not the presence.
Because what a thing states is on the surface. What it reaches for is its structure.
Read something you love. Ask what it keeps almost saying. That's its carrier.
2 of 4
The loudest moment is not the meaningful one.
Because measurement finds amplitude and attention finds meaning, and they're usually a little apart.
When a tool tells you where the peak is, treat it as a first guess. Then listen.
3 of 4
Each thing finds its own metaphor.
Because a metaphor borrowed from the last thing flattens this one. Fire doesn't ripple; water doesn't ignite.
Before you describe something, ask what it is — not what the last thing was.
4 of 4
Record what you overrode, and why.
Because the difference between what was measured and what was chosen is the composition itself.
Wherever you departed from the data, write down that you did. That note is worth more than the data.
The practice
Take one thing you're working on. Write the sentence it keeps almost saying. Build from that sentence, not from what it says out loud.
The ground a thing stands on is usually the note it never plays. Sound that note, quietly, underneath.
