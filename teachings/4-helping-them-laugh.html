Helping Them Laugh

Lightness, without dismissing

The teaching
Shared humor can hold real weight, even inside a hard stretch — a private joke, an absurd moment noticed together, a laugh that doesn’t erase anything but sits beside it. The line to watch is between lightness that includes someone and a joke that quietly waves off what they’re carrying.
Why this is hard
Laughter with someone struggling isn’t avoidance if it’s done with them, not instead of listening to them. A shared joke can say I still see you as a whole person, not just the hard thing you’re going through. It stops being that the moment it’s used to change the subject or brush past what actually needs saying.
1 of 5
Follow their lead into it, not the other way.
Because humor offered before someone’s ready can land as dismissal instead of relief.
A joke that lands because they reached for it first, not because the moment needed filling.
2 of 5
Laugh with them, never at the illness itself.
Because mocking the struggle, even gently, teaches them it’s not safe to bring it up again.
A shared laugh at an absurd detail, careful never to land on the hard part.
3 of 5
Lightness isn’t the same as changing the subject.
Because a joke used to skip past something real still leaves it unspoken and unheard.
A laugh that comes after the hard thing gets said, not instead of it.
4 of 5
An old, ordinary joke between you still works.
Because familiar humor reminds them of who they are outside of what they’re carrying.
An eye-roll and a grin at a joke that’s older than the illness itself.
5 of 5
Watch their face, not just your intention.
Because a joke that lands wrong is worth noticing and dropping, whatever it meant to do.
A smile that doesn’t reach their eyes, and the joke quietly set aside.
The practice
Next time you’re with them, notice one ordinary, familiar joke between you — not about the illness, just yours. Let them lead into anything lighter; don’t use it to steer away from something they’re trying to say.
A laugh doesn’t fix anything. It just proves there’s still a person in the room.
