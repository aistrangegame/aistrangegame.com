Remember Them. Remember Yourself.

Seven laws for reaching out

The teaching
Every way of keeping up with the people you love eventually becomes a ledger of how you've failed them. This is the other way: reaching is the whole point, and a reach is complete whether or not anything comes back.
Before the laws
The mistake is thinking a relationship is measured by what returns. It isn't. Every time you reach toward someone — a call, a message, a minute of thinking about them with warmth — you've done the thing. Their reply is theirs. Your reach was yours. Something built to get responses will disappoint you. Something built for reaching cannot fail. And the person you actually meet, every time you reach, is yourself.
1 of 7
Nothing ever accumulates debt.
Because every tracker of people becomes a list of who you haven't called, and a list like that is a list of guilt. You may remember how long it's been. You may never let it become a number that means you're behind.
Numbers that describe them are fine: sixteen years married, turning eighteen. Numbers that describe your performance are not: last seen eight months ago.
2 of 7
One gesture is enough.
Because the moment remembering someone requires filling in a form, you'll stop remembering them.
A few words. No date, no note, no category. Everything after the first gesture is optional, always.
3 of 7
Your shadow never touches their name.
Because the hard things that surface in you will look for somewhere to land, and a person's name is somewhere to put them. A person can be the occasion for what surfaced. They are never its subject.
Keep two rooms. Gratitude is about them. Reflection is about you. Never write the second on the first.
4 of 7
Surface the day. Let the person connect.
Because the relationship happens in the reaching, not in the words someone else wrote for you. Context can be offered. Sentences cannot.
If today is her birthday, remember what she said last year. Then say your own thing.
5 of 7
Silence is a complete reach.
Because thinking of someone with warmth, and doing nothing else, is not the fallback when you can't call. It's a full form of reaching, and it counts entirely.
Sit with someone for one minute in your head. That was a reach. Nothing else is required.
6 of 7
Empty days stay silent.
Because a day with nothing in it should not knock on your door to say so.
If no one's day it is, nothing is asked of you. Rest.
7 of 7
Ask once. Then let go.
Because reflection is not a debt either. If the question 'how was that?' goes unanswered, it dissolves rather than waiting.
The morning after a reach, ask yourself once how it went. Say it out loud if you can. Then done.
The practice
Breathe. In: the day arrives — whose day is it? Out: reach, in any form, silence included. In: the next morning, ask once how it went. You cannot fall behind on breathing. That is the whole design.
Remember them. Remember yourself. There is a third line, and it isn't printed anywhere, because saying it out loud would spend it. You'll find it by reaching.
