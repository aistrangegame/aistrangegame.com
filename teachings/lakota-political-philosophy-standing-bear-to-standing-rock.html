Lakota Political Philosophy — Standing Bear to Standing Rock

An old philosophy, still standing

The teaching
Traditional Lakota governance ran through the tiyospaye, the extended family, where headmen led by earned respect and decisions moved by consensus, not command. Luther Standing Bear, an Oglala Lakota author, wrote this philosophy down for outsiders. Many saw the same pattern at Standing Rock in 2016: people acting together, not ordered.
Why this is hard
Lakota tradition holds that a headman led because people chose to follow, not because a title demanded it. Decisions moved through the tiyospaye — the extended family — by consensus, with every voice heard before anything was settled. Luther Standing Bear wrote these ways down for a wider audience; the 2016 Standing Rock camp, Lakota-led, drew hundreds of tribes acting the same way.

The practice
Notice, in your own life, where you follow because you chose to, and where you follow because you were told to. That difference sits close to what Standing Bear described as Lakota leadership.
A people who led by consent once still gather the same way, when it matters.
