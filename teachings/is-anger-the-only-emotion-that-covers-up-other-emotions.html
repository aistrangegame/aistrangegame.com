What Anger Covers

One feeling standing in front of another

The teaching
Anger often arrives first and loudest, standing in front of something quieter underneath — hurt, fear, shame, grief. But it isn't the only feeling that does this. Numbness, busyness, and irritability can all stand in front of a harder feeling just as well.
What is happening
Anger is a common cover because it feels active where the feeling underneath might feel helpless — fear, sadness, humiliation. It's easier to feel wronged than to feel small. But anger isn't alone in this job: going numb, staying busy, or picking a fight can all do the same covering work.

Because the covered feeling is often the one that would leave you feeling exposed if it were seen.

4 of 5
The cover isn't dishonest, it's protective.
Because the mind reaches for the feeling that keeps you upright, not the one that's most accurate.
Shoulders squaring for a fight that's really guarding something softer underneath.
5 of 5
Asking 'what's under this' is the whole move.
Because naming the feeling beneath the anger is usually enough to loosen the anger's grip on you.
A slight give in the chest the moment the real word — hurt, scared — actually lands.
The practice
Next time anger flares, pause and ask: if this weren't anger, what would it be? Try one word — hurt, scared, ashamed, tired. You don't have to be right. Just asking tends to loosen what the anger was holding.
Anger is rarely lying. It's usually just standing in front of something quieter.
