Bindu Learning

One teacher at a time, slowly

In one sentence
An app for studying one big topic at a time — a teacher, a text, a tradition, a piece of science — in five short movements, with a few days of waiting between chapters on purpose.
A Tuesday
Tuesday I read a chapter on the Gita — the door, one sentence that lets me in, then the first real teaching. I mark a line that lands. The app locks the next chapter for three days. Thursday, something from that line shows up at work without my planning it. Saturday the next chapter arrives, and I read it differently.
Why I made it
I've always learned fast and forgotten faster. I wanted an instrument that made me live with a teaching for a few ordinary days before giving me the next piece — so the teaching had to meet my actual life, not just my attention.
What's inside
Two hundred topics, from Jung to the Gita to plant intelligence to CERN.
Every topic has the same arc: a door, the knowledge in five steps, a few real voices from history, a synthesis, and a threshold that links it back to my own life.
Three things you can do while reading: mark it, reflect on it, or link it to something you already are.
What it refuses to do
It won't deliver information. It makes room and steps back.
It won't let you binge. The waiting is the mechanism.
If you want to try it
Mine, for now. A topic or two may become readable here later.
