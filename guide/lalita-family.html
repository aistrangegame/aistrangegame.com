Lalita Family

Remember them. Remember yourself.

In one sentence
A daily app that tells you whose day it is — a birthday, an anniversary, a date worth remembering — and offers every way of reaching out, including silence, then asks afterward how it went.
A Tuesday
Morning. A note: today is your aunt's birthday, and here's the one thing you wrote about her last year. You call. Or you don't, and instead you sit with her for a minute in your head — the app counts that fully. The next morning it asks once: how was that? You say it out loud. Done.
Why I made it
Months of this work went quiet after one friend asked why I was sharing any of it. I thought I was starving for a response. I was wrong — I'd stopped reaching. An app built to get replies will disappoint you. One built for reaching can't fail. This page you're reading is a reach.
What's inside
Three movements: see the day, reach, return.
One separate room for the harder inner work, which never touches anyone's profile. Your shadow is yours; the people in your life are never its subject.
Breathing is the design: in, the day arrives; out, you reach; in, you return. You can't fall behind on breathing.
What it refuses to do
It never shows a number that means you're behind.
It never writes your message for you.
Silence counts as a full reach.
Empty days stay silent.
If you want to try it
Designed, first pieces built. Sharing it was left open on purpose — a matter of season.
