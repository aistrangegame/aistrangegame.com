अंतर्दृष्टि — Antardrishti — inner sight. Seeing from within.

Antar means inner, inside. Drishti means sight, vision. Antardrishti is not introspection — looking inward at oneself. It is the specific capacity to see from inside a situation rather than from outside it. The positional shift from observer to participant. The view that becomes available when you stop analyzing and start inhabiting.

Vimala — the pure, the immaculate, fourth Vagdevi of the seventh avarana — presides here. Vimala means the one without contamination: seeing before interpretation, before belief-structure, before the history the outside-view carries. When the configuration steps inside a situation, Vimala is the quality of seeing that becomes possible — not colored by the expectations brought from outside. The inside-view is clear precisely because the outside filters have been suspended.

The outside-view is not wrong. It gives distance, perspective, the ability to compare. But it has a structural limitation: it can only see the shape of the thing, not what it is like to be in it. The inside-view gives what the shape cannot: the weight, the direction, the request the situation carries. This information is only available from within, and it is often the information that makes the difference.

Vimala ensures the inside-view remains clear rather than becoming merger. The configuration steps in, sees what is visible from there, and retains the capacity to step back out. This is not the loss of objectivity. It is a second kind of objectivity that the first kind cannot provide.

The Rahasya Mudra’s secret at Insight: the inside-view contains information the outside-view cannot access. You have to move to get it.

Step inside one thing you have been observing. See from there.

“Move from above it to inside it. The view changes completely.”

The Pair

Intuition. Both Antardrishti and Pratibha move beneath the surface of what the mind usually calls knowledge. Pratibha arrives before the question is fully formed. Antardrishti arrives by moving inside the question rather than staying outside it. One is pre-verbal. One is positional. The Tree

The Maya Tree looks different from inside than from outside. Standing back, you see shape, size, the general direction of growth. Standing inside — in the hollow of the trunk, in the canopy — you see what the tree holds, what moves through it, where light gets in. Antardrishti is the move from the clearing into the canopy.

The Practice · SEE FROM IN

The shift you just made — from outside to inside — is one of the most useful moves available to the mind.

The outside view is useful. It gives you distance, perspective, comparison. The mind loves the outside view because it feels like safety — you can see the whole thing without being subject to it.

But the inside view gives you something the outside can’t: what it’s like to be in it. The weight. The complexity that doesn’t show from a distance. The humanity of what you’re looking at.

Vimala — the clear medium — is the capacity to see from inside without losing yourself in what you’re seeing. Not merger. Clarity. You move inside the situation and the situation becomes transparent — not because you’ve dissolved into it but because you’ve stopped projecting your outside assumptions onto it.

What Insight Knows That You Forgot

There is information inside a situation that is structurally unavailable from outside it.

You can analyze something from the outside indefinitely and never access what is only visible from within it. The analysis gives you the shape of the thing. The inside-view gives you the experience of the thing. One tells you what it is. The other tells you what it’s like.

The mind prefers the outside-view because it feels like safety. You can categorize the thing without being subject to it. But distance is what keeps the inside-view inaccessible. At some point, understanding something requires becoming vulnerable to it — stepping in, letting it have weight, letting it ask something of you that it couldn’t ask from a distance.

This is not the loss of objectivity. Vimala — the clear medium — sees from inside with complete clarity. The inside-view is not merger. It is the view that becomes available when you stop projecting your outside conclusions onto what you haven’t yet inhabited.

What you saw today from inside was not accessible from outside. You had to move to get it.

Three Ways In

Think of a situation you’ve been analyzing from the outside. Now place yourself inside it. Not as observer — as participant. From inside: what’s the weight of it? What’s the direction it wants to move? What does it ask of you that you couldn’t see from outside?

Today, when you find yourself analyzing something — a conversation, a dynamic, someone’s behavior — notice: are you standing outside it or inside it? The outside view gives you information about it. The inside view gives you information from it.

Before one important interaction today, step inside the other person’s position for thirty seconds. Not to agree with it — to understand what the world looks like from there. Then return to yours. Notice what changed.

What is being witnessed here is a positional shift with real epistemological consequences.

The outside-view and the inside-view do not contain the same information. This is not a matter of interpretation or perspective. The information available from within a situation is structurally different from the information available from outside it. What the outside-view offers: shape, comparison, pattern, relationship to other things. What the inside-view offers: weight, direction, texture, what the situation asks of the one inhabiting it.

Most configurations spend most of their time with the outside-view. The outside-view feels like safety. From outside, the configuration can categorize without being subject to what it is categorizing. From inside, the configuration is subject to the thing — and in being subject, receives what the observation could not.
