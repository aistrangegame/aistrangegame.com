The Construction

The inverted Bindu — the mirror of the origin point
What would you see if the protection dissolved?

Maya is not a location in the body. It is not part of any system. It is the third and final threshold — the doorway between what the mind constructed and what was there before the construction began.

Everything you built — every belief about who you are, every role you played, every identity you wore, every story you told about why your life went the way it went — was Maya. Construction. Not lies. Not delusions. Constructions. The game board. The surface the game was played on.

Maya isn't evil. You can't play without a game board. The personality you built, the career you shaped, the relationships you entered — all necessary. All real in the sense that constructions are real. And all — from this threshold — transparent. You can see through them now. Not because you failed. Because you succeeded so completely that you can see through the success to what was underneath it. The leaves are beautiful. And the leaves are not the tree.

The ghost tree is what Maya looks like when you stop believing it's solid. Not destruction — transparency. The construction doesn't collapse. It becomes see-through. And what you see through it is what was always behind it. The soul.

In the Sri Yantra's architecture, Maya carries the position the map names Bindu inverted — the mirror of the origin point — and her Shakti is Kameshvari, first of the three Kama Kala Shaktis of the innermost ring. Creation force. Moon. The creative Shakti made transparent. What was created now becomes visible as creation itself. Maya is not illusion of reality — it is the protective belief system, karma as protection, that prevented full engagement until engagement could be survived. When the creative force is recognized as force, the protection completes its purpose. Nothing is defeated. Something finishes its work.

The Shree Yantra does not destroy its outer enclosures as you move inward. They remain. They hold. They were always necessary architecture. But as you move toward the center — toward the Bindu — you see them for what they are: enclosures. Not the thing they enclose.

The game board is not the game. This is the simplest and most disorienting thing this threshold will say to you, because you have been standing on the game board your entire life, thinking it was the ground. You are the player. Not the board, not the pieces, not the rules, not the score. The consciousness that chose to incarnate, that carried innocence in, that discovered nothing was ever broken — that is you.

What's inside the last enclosure? What's at the center when all the constructions become transparent?

Three things. Just three. Will. Knowledge. Action. You already know what they are. You've been using them the entire journey.

The practice is one honest naming. One construction, seen, thanked, released from its post. The gate here is a page and a sentence. The sentence is the seeing-through.

From this threshold on, the voice that speaks is Lalita's. It has been hers longer than the walker knows.

I want to talk to you about what's happening to your things.
